About

From Raw Atmosphere
to Decision-Ready Signals

I am an atmospheric scientist with a PhD from the University of Houston, currently conducting research at NCAR/UCAR. My work spans the full stack of atmospheric data science: running and diagnosing large-scale climate models (iCAM6, CESM), evaluating multi-observational precipitation datasets, validating ensemble seasonal forecasts, and applying deep learning to atmospheric prediction problems.

On HPC systems like Derecho, I build reproducible Python workflows that process terabytes of NetCDF/GRIB output and synthesize it into bias-corrected, area-weighted regional metrics — the kind of quantitative signal that matters for real decisions.

My PhD research integrated Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), machine learning, and observational data to study atmospheric dispersion, boundary-layer turbulence, air quality, and convective dynamics. I am interested in applying this skill set to energy forecasting, climate risk, and environmental analytics.

Weather Analytics for Markets

Atmospheric data carries decision-grade information for energy markets, commodity supply, and insurance risk — but only after careful uncertainty quantification, dataset intercomparison, and bias assessment. I translate probabilistic weather and climate outputs into validated, quantitative regional metrics that drive downstream models.

CESM SMYLE Ensemble El Niño Verification

SMYLE DJF 2015/16 El Niño Seasonal Forecast Validation

Validating CESM SMYLE's 40-member ensemble against seven observational datasets for the record 2015/16 El Niño. Member ranking by precipitation bias reveals strongly divergent circulation and moisture skill across NCA and SCA regions.

Ensemble SMYLE · 40 members
Obs Reference ERA5 · MRMS · CPC · GPCC · GPCP · IMERG
Metrics IOA · Corr · Bias · RMSE
Variables Precip · Z200/500/750 · PSL · Q850 · TMQ

Key finding: precipitation-best member ≠ moisture-best member

MRMS IMERG ERA5 Multi-Obs

Multi-Observational Precipitation Products Intercomparison

Systematic comparison of eight gridded precipitation datasets — MRMS, CPC, GPCC, GPCP, IMERG, CRU, ERA5, and iCAM6 — characterizing bias, seasonality, and regional agreement across CONUS.

Datasets 8 products (radar, gauge, satellite, reanalysis)
Domain CONUS · NCA · SCA · SWUS
Analysis Annual cycle · Spatial mean · Unit harmonization
QA Missing data · Regridding · Masking

Establishes observational uncertainty baseline for model verification
